About this experiment

Imagine a very small community in which people were raised speaking only Klingon. A patient visits an English-speaking physician and needs an interpreter. The interpreter happens to be a member of that same small community, and learns something intensely personal about the patient.

Even if the arrangement meets the letter of HIPAA, the patient may feel that the spirit of privacy has been lost. The interpreter may be entirely trustworthy, yet the patient may still hesitate to speak freely.

Could an artificial interpreter someday provide an additional layer of social privacy? This is one motivation for the experiment. AI introduces its own risks involving accuracy, data processing and confidentiality. This prototype is not a claim of HIPAA compliance, nor a replacement for a qualified medical interpreter.
